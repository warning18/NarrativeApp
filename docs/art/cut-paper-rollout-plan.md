# Cut paper: how to extend it (plan, nothing generated; Art, 25 November 2026)

The user approved cut paper as the direction (the second try: ink, paper, a paper shadow, one clan colour). What worked in the second try: **text-only prompts for flat subjects** (the fire, the dice) and a **very light init (strength about 80)** when an emblem must survive (the banner). Costs are in generations: one per piece, plus about 30 % for retries. Balance 1751. Rule: ask the user before any batch above about 30.

## Suggested order and running cost
1. Dice and the six banners first (steps 1 and 2): small, visible everywhere, and they set the look: **12 generations, 17 with retries**.
2. The Tower backgrounds (step 3): the Tower is where the user moves toward the new style: **14, about 19**. Running total about 36.
3. Foe heads, test of 4 (step 4): fixes the faceless heads at the same time: **4, about 6**.
4. Icon pilot (step 5), then the icon waves (step 6): the biggest line; do the pilot, look, then ask for the first wave: **8, then waves of about 70**.
5. Fight plates and scenes (steps 7 and 8) last: the most generations for the least change of identity.

## Notes
- The Tower's clan offer cards keep the story's banner card style (UX's), so no new frames are planned for them; step 2 gives the banners the card shows.
- Palette, the same for every step and free after the call: `art/pixellab-brut/` snap to ink 090A14, a dark 28283A, paper shadow C9C2A6, paper E7E1CD and one clan hue plus a dark of it; a plate or a scene may carry two or three of the clan hues, but no piece uses the warning red A53030 except for blood and warnings.
- Risks seen in the second try: the model adds a little 3D shading to cubes (dice), keeps a grey ground on banners (keyed out in code), and drifts from the emblem if the init strength is below about 60.
- The paper doll stays on hold.

## Step 3, the Tower plates, first try (25 November): 14 generations, balance 1704; STOPPED here
The 14 plates, each text plus the current plate as an init at strength 90, seed 41, cut-paper prompt, then snapped to ink, dark, paper shadow, paper and a deep red (red kept only where it was red). Sheet `docs/art/boards/cutpaper-tower-plates.png` (today above, cut paper below, two rows of seven); files `art/pixellab-cutpaper/tower/final/tower_<id>.png` (180 x 320 PNG8), raw in `art/pixellab-cutpaper/tower/raw/`.
**Result: it is not cut paper.** At strength 90 the model keeps the stones, the brickwork and the textures of the plate, so the snapped result is a busy, grainy stone drawing in black and cream, with none of the big flat shapes and hard layer shadows of the fire and the dice. The init carries the detail; the second try of the fire and the dice worked because it had no init. So the plates need either **no init** (text only: the composition changes, the style holds) or a very low strength (about 40: composition roughly kept, still some detail), at another 14 generations (19 with retries). Per the Manager's rule, the foe heads, the tarot and the icon test were not started.

## Step 3, the method test (25 November): 6 generations, balance 1698; stopped, the user picks
Three plates (band 1, boss 10, ward 15), each (a) text only and (b) the plate as an init at strength 40. Sheet `docs/art/boards/cutpaper-tower-compare.png` (today | a | b for each, raw, before any palette snap); raw in `art/pixellab-cutpaper/tower/raw/<id>_a`, `_b`.
- **(a) text only is the better method.** It gives big flat shapes, strong hard light and shadow, and bold red banners (boss 10 with torn red banners and a dark doorway; band 1 a curved stair under a cream light shaft); it reads as layered paper. The composition is new (not the current plate's), and it sometimes uses more than one accent (the ward's orange glow, the band's brown stone): a palette snap to ink, dark, paper shadow, paper and one red would pull it in.
- **(b) init 40 is worse:** busier brick, less bold, the ward almost empty grey; it keeps some composition but loses the look.
- So for the rest of the plates: text only, with a prompt per plate (what the scene is), 14 generations (19 with retries), then the palette snap; the new plates replace the old ones rather than redrawing them.
