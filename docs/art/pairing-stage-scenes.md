# Pairing: fight stage against story places, and the consistency check (text; 0 generations)
The user's rule: **a place looks the same in its fight stage and in its story scenes** (same architecture and materials, palette and accent, hour and weather, landmark props). This page pairs each `FightStage` with the story locations whose fights use it, says whether the chosen finals agree, and lists the redo count. Sheet: `docs/art/boards/consistency-stage-vs-bands.png` (a stage plate, then three of its places' bands, rows of 4).
## How a location's stage was found
`lib/data/geography.dart` (`stageNameOf`): the nearest `stage` written on the place or the places above it, else the land's biome through `fightStageOf` (`field_anomaly.dart`): fields, dunes, saltFlats, waves, ash, terraces, cliffs, reeds, snow, glass by biome; a building is a hall; a town's street is cityRoad. I resolved each of the 74 `location` ids through the geography chain (explicit `stage`, building level, town ancestor, else the biome). It is my reading of the data, not a run of the game: **ask Core to confirm** for any place that matters.

## What the check finds
The stage plates are one per stage, but a stage is used by many places of different regions (38 halls, 20 city roads). A single plate cannot match them all. Mismatches that the user would see (a stage plate against its place's band): **cityRoad** (4 regions: Alster warm timber, Saltmouth white salt-bleached, the grey frost of the Reliquary and the Ashen Quarter, Candlehold's white stone), **glass** (cyan against a brown dusk), **ash** (grey against orange), **terraces** (brown against a red haze), **waves** (day against the night crossing), **hall** (wet vaults), **snow**/**cliffs** (palette, hour), plus **stage mismatches**: lark_wreck (a sea wreck on a dunes stage), highhearth (arid stone on a snow stage).

## From now on
Each new band is generated from its paired stage's words (materials, palette, hour), and each new stage variant from its place's band, with the neighbour as a style hint in the prompt (the tool takes an init image but follows it too tightly for a different composition, so the words carry the match).
