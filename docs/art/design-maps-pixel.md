# Ashen Quarter and casino in real pixel art: two approaches (picture first, nothing wired)
The user kept today's maps and asked for improved pixel art, today's layout kept. Sheets: `docs/art/boards/map-pixel-ashen.png`, `map-pixel-casino.png`, and `map-pixel-casino-align.png` (the plan's walls, doors and wheel drawn over the generated plates to check alignment). Files: `art/pixellab-maps-pixel/` (`a/` the tileset renders and `tileset_sheet.png`, `b/raw/` the six generations, `guide_*` the init images, generators `tileset_gen.py`, `b_gen.py`, `plan_geometry.py`).
**Cost: 6 generations** (balance 1122.5 to about 1116.5); approach A is hand-drawn, 0.

## A. Tileset laid on the plan (drawn, 0 generations)
An 8 px top-down tileset in the art direction (ink outline, raw clusters, three variants of each tile so repeats do not look stamped): cobbles, ash ground, rubble, burnt roofs seen from above (house, hall, temple, stall; soot, embers, collapsed roofs), stone wall and towers, dark planks, red carpet, felt, vault slate, plus standing lanterns, chips and candles, chandeliers. The same renderer lays it on the game's own plan polygons, so **alignment is exact by construction** (every wall, door, gate, building and anchor is the plan's own). Both maps show it: the town reads as a burnt walled town with embers and lanterns, the casino as a dark plank hall with a red runner, felt tables, candle chandeliers and a bar.
- **Pro:** exact alignment, 0 cost, **reusable for every town and every room** (new tiles are added once, then any plan is drawn), consistent across the whole game, easy to tint per environment later.
- **Con:** a tiled look; hand-made tiles need a polish pass for variety and a few more tile types (water, grass, doors), and the town reads flat at the edges between districts.
## B. Painted plate from today's layout (img2img, `create_image_pixflux`, 1 generation a try)
Init image = today's plate (the flat v1 of #938), the details prompted (burnt roofs, embers, rubble, lanterns / dark wood floor, red runner, felt tables, candles), three init strengths (80, 120, 170) per map.
- **Casino: works.** Strengths 120 and 170 keep the layout (walls, doors, wheel line up with the plan overlay) and add real painting: bottles on the bar, a wheel with red and black pockets, stools, candles. Strength 80 paints a richer room (wall faces, candle holders, stools) but adds a wall face at the top, so its rooms sit a few pixels lower than the plan. Best candidates: v1 (120) for exact alignment, v2 (80) for richness.
- **Ashen Quarter: fails.** At 120 and 80 the model invents a different town (a ring of fire pits, a fortress seen in perspective); at 170 it keeps the roads but drops most buildings into brown dots. A 171-building plan is beyond what the generator keeps from a guide.
- **Pro:** the richest single picture; 1 generation. **Con:** one plate per room, no reuse, alignment must be checked each time, and it cannot hold a dense town.
## Recommendation
**A as the base for towns and rooms** (exact, free, reusable, scales to every other town), and **B only for a few hero rooms** where a painted plate is worth 1 to 3 generations, starting with the casino (v1) after a check against the plan overlay. For the Ashen Quarter, A only. Next, if approved: a polish pass on the tileset (more variants, water and grass tiles, door tiles, a night tint) and a hand-off (tile size 8 px, scale 0.5 plan units per pixel, placement as in `art/pixellab-maps/placement.json`).
